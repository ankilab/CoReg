CoReg

Luisa Neubig, Moritz Palm, Alessio Attardo, Joseph C. Donovan, Daniil Markov, Peter Soba, Neena Dihman, Shinjini Ghosh, Peter Rupprecht, and Andreas M. Kist

Motion correction for in vivo calcium imaging. Point-based tracking (CoTracker) combined with sparse-to-dense warping — a GUI, a benchmark CLI, self-supervised finetuning, and analysis tools.

Abstract

In vivo calcium imaging enables the observation of neural activity with high spatial and temporal resolution. However, recordings are frequently affected by motion artifacts caused by animal movement, physiological processes, tissue drift, or instability of the imaging setup. These artifacts make signal extraction harder and reduce the consistency of downstream analyses. Current registration methods can correct rigid and non-rigid motion, but often estimate motion from individual frames or image patches and make limited use of temporal information. In addition, activity-driven fluorescence changes can make it difficult to distinguish changes in image appearance from actual motion.

Here, we treat motion correction in calcium imaging as a point tracking problem and introduce CoReg, an open-source Python-based software tool with a graphical user interface. We benchmark CoReg against established registration methods using fluorescence imaging datasets from multiple species. CoReg performs competitively with or better than existing approaches across several settings. We further show that its performance is robust to the choice of tracked points, making the method straightforward to apply. CoReg includes implementations of both our method and the evaluated established methods. Our results show that tracking-based registration provides a simple and reliable approach for motion correction in in vivo calcium imaging.

Results

CoReg's performance is robust to the choice of tracked points: the clip below runs all six point presets — from a fixed 16×16 grid to feature-, segmentation-, activity-, and matching-based point selection, with wildly different point counts and layouts — on the same strong-movement two-photon calcium recording. Despite that, every preset converges to a comparably stable, well-aligned motion-corrected result.

All six CoReg point presets side by side — tracked points (top) and motion-corrected output (bottom), same recording

The two clips below are from the same recording. The left video compares CoReg's regular-grid preset side by side against four established baselines (ANTs, LDDMM, PyFlowReg, NoRMCorre); CoReg keeps cell bodies sharp and in place throughout the recording without any method-specific tuning. The right video zooms into a single soma and overlays its per-method ΔF/F₀ activity trace, making residual motion artifacts — spurious spikes that track frame displacement rather than real activity — directly visible in the unregistered trace and largely absent after registration.

Features

GUI

Load a video/TIFF/HDF5 file, pick a point preset, run registration interactively with live preview.

Benchmark CLI

Compare CoTracker presets against classical baselines across a whole folder of recordings, with per-video and per-frame CSV metrics.

Analysis tools

Recompute registration-quality metrics, annotate cell activity (ΔF/F) or area, and overlay registered channels interactively.

Finetuning

Test-time-adapt CoTracker to your own recordings via a self-supervised cycle-consistency loss -- no manual point-track labels needed.

Quick start

Install

git clone https://github.com/neuluna/registration_gui.git
cd registration_gui

pip install torch torchvision --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu124  # adjust to your driver
pip install -e .

GUI

coreg-gui

Benchmark CLI

coreg-cli --folder /path/to/videos --output-dir /path/to/out --run-name my_run --num-frames 50 --save-videos

coreg-baseline --folder /path/to/videos --methods normcorre_pwrigid pyflowreg lddmm ants \
               --output-dir /path/to/out --run-name baseline_run --num-frames 50

Full option sets, repository layout, and the finetuning/analysis workflows are in the README.
